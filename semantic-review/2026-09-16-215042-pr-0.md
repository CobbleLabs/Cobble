# SilentAura publication snapshot and reach-validation hardening

SilentAura now serializes published yaw, pitch, validity, packet serial, and publication revision behind one mutex, then revalidates that snapshot and keeps it stable until the attack packet is enqueued. Competing rotation owners invalidate through the same mutex, while C02 attack and C0A swing packets fail the C03 type check before they can reacquire it. The 0.02 per-axis AABB tolerance rejects Reach's minimum 0.05 horizontal displacement before slab intersection. No new actionable defect was found in duplicate-look handling or slab/reach validation.

Watch for: **confirmed, non-blocking** — the publication interleavings and 0.02/0.05 boundary have no automated regression coverage.

**Verdict**: APPROVED

## High-level view

The publication mutex now covers every field that proves which server rotation an attack relies on. The publication revision and packet serial detect changes during visibility and ray checks, then the mutex remains held while `attackEntity` emits its swing and attack packets.

Both SilentAura rewriting and competing-owner invalidation perform the C03 instance check before taking the mutex. Nested C0A/C02 packets therefore return without locking, while a competing C03 waits until attack enqueue completes and invalidates afterward.

A duplicate managed look sets `rotating=false` while retaining the previously published server look, and still advances packet freshness. Competing invalidation clears validity, forcing the next SilentAura-owned C03 to publish yaw and pitch even if their values match the old look.

The AABB gate requires the live box center to remain within 0.02 of entity position before the 2.90-block slab and occlusion checks. Reach's 0.05 shift has at least one horizontal component of about 0.035 or greater, so the new tolerance rejects even a diagonal minimum displacement.

## Published rotation remains coherent through attack enqueue

The publication state is read under `publicationMutex_` as one snapshot: validity, revision, packet serial, yaw, and pitch. After visibility and geometry checks, SilentAura reacquires the mutex and rejects the attack if validity cleared or either revision changed. It consumes the serial and calls `mc.attackEntity` before unlocking, preventing publication or competing-C03 invalidation between the final check and enqueue.

```text
owned C03
  -> onOutgoingPacket [publicationMutex]
     -> publish yaw/pitch/validity
     -> advance packet serial + revision

module update
  -> snapshot [publicationMutex]
  -> visibility + AABB/slab checks
  -> revalidate serial/revision [publicationMutex]
  -> consume serial
  -> enqueue swing C0A + attack C02
  -> unlock

competing C03
  -> C03 type check
  -> wait for publicationMutex
  -> clear validity + advance revision
```

`attackEntity` synchronously calls `swingItem` and the player controller, producing C0A and C02 traffic. `onOutgoingPacket` and `onCompetingOutgoingPacket` both test C03 membership before acquiring `publicationMutex_`, so those nested callbacks cannot wait on the mutex held by the attacking thread. A competing C03 on another writer blocks until enqueue returns. **Confirmed:** the prior incoherent-snapshot and C02-deadlock blockers are resolved in the inspected paths.

## Duplicate C03 publication preserves the server-side look

When managed yaw and pitch are byte-identical to a valid publication, SilentAura clears the packet's `rotating` bit and retains the prior yaw, pitch, and validity because the server keeps its last look. It still increments the movement serial and revision after earlier packet gates accept the C03, giving the attack path fresh packet evidence without claiming a new look was sent.

Competing rotation invalidates that retained look under the mutex. Because `changed` includes `!publishedValid`, the next SilentAura-owned C03 writes yaw and pitch and sets `rotating=true` even if the values match the old publication. **Confirmed:** no stale-validity or duplicate-look bypass was found.

## AABB provenance check rejects Reach before slab validation

Reach moves the AABB center by `dist * (cos(angle), sin(angle))` in 0.05 increments. A non-zero 0.05 vector has at least one component of `0.05 / sqrt(2)`, approximately 0.035, or larger; SilentAura rejects either horizontal center delta above 0.02. Axis-aligned and diagonal minimum Reach offsets are therefore excluded.

Finite coordinates, player-like positive dimensions, and a plausible vertical anchor are required before the slab calculation clips all three axes to a 2.90 maximum and block-raytraces to the first box intersection. **Confirmed:** no new actionable defect was found in box-shape, slab-entry, reach-cap, or block-occlusion validation.
